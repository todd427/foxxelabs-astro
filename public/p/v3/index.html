edit_note

Design decisions

What changed from v2 and why

close

Killed the grid

The 7-column 2·4·5·8·5·4·2 forced grid is researcher tooling. Showing it to the participant made them learn methodology to do the task. Now hidden — participant taps faces, server snaps to grid behind the scenes.

sentiment_satisfied

Five faces, not seven thumbs

😠 🙁 😐 🙂 😄 reads in <1s. 👎👎👎 vs 👎👎 vs 👎 needed a decoder ring. Five points is what humans natively do on Likert.

format_size

One statement, big and centered

v2 showed all 30 sentences as a wall. v3 shows one at a time, 1.8rem font, breathing room around it. You can read it on a phone in portrait without zooming.

accessibility_new

Pinch-zoom re-enabled

v2 had maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=no — that disables every browser accessibility feature. Removed.

edit

Intro is a question, not a method explainer

"What do you actually think about phones in schools?" instead of "A Q-sort is a research method for finding distinct viewpoints…" People care about the question, not the technique.

person

Result shows the participant their own answers

"Your three strongest agreements" gives them ownership of the result. It’s reflecting back what they actually said, not labelling them.

Honest caveat (researcher-facing)

This is a 5-point Likert rated card-by-card, then snapped server-side onto a 2·4·5·8·5·4·2 distribution. It produces Q-sort-shaped output without requiring participants to actively rank within buckets. For 6th Form classroom use this is the right trade-off. For dissertation work you’d run a proper rank-within-bucket Q on a smaller, more motivated sample. Both raw 5-point data and snapped grid positions get stored, so cross-validation is possible.
