## The Big Picture: More Jobs Created Than Lost, But With Caveats

Gartner forecasts that artificial intelligence will generate **10.6 million new jobs globally by 2030** while simultaneously eliminating **8.9 million jobs**, resulting in a net increase of **1.7 million jobs**. The findings were presented at the Gartner IT Symposium/Xpo 2026, held in Barcelona, Spain, on 10 November 2026.

While the overall outlook appears positive, Gartner's analysis reveals significant structural shifts and timing mismatches that will create friction in labour markets across Europe and beyond.

## Tech Sector Dominates Job Creation

Of the net 1.7 million job increase, approximately **1.5 million (89%) will come from the tech sector alone**. This concentration signals where investment and growth are being channeled, but also highlights uneven opportunity distribution across industries.

In the software and services segment specifically, Gartner projects **up to 2 million jobs gained and approximately 402,000 jobs lost** attributable to AI going forward. According to Gartner Distinguished VP Analyst **Helen Poitevin**, this segment has already passed a critical inflection point: "Software and services have already passed the inflection point where AI is creating more jobs than it is eliminating in that segment."

Poitevin also noted that **only 1.8% of roles in the tech sector will actually be eliminated by AI**, suggesting most displacement concerns are overblown in absolute terms.

## The Skills Shift: Programmers Face Pressure

However, the picture is more nuanced when examining specific job categories. **Traditional 'computer programmer' positions are declining while demand is surging for software and AI engineers who can manage AI and orchestrate agents.** This represents not so much a net job loss as a rapid recomposition of the technical workforce—a challenge for career planning and training programmes across Ireland and the EU.

## The Hiring Delay Problem

A major wildcard in Gartner's analysis: the forecast that **over five million people worldwide will experience delayed hiring by 2030 as enterprises defer job creation under the assumption that AI can fill workforce gaps.**

Poitevin warned that this deferral may prove short-sighted. "Because expectations of AI capabilities outpace reality, companies will eventually face rounds of 'corrective hiring' as employees tackle increased workloads," she explained. This suggests that even if net job creation is positive, the *timing* of opportunity creation will be uneven—creating a squeeze period for job seekers.

## Entry-Level Workers Face Immediate Pressure

**Immediate friction from AI-driven labour displacement is falling hardest on entry-level workers**, according to Gartner. This finding has significant implications for graduate employment and apprenticeship pathways across European economies, where youth employment rates are already a policy concern.

## Secondary Employment from Infrastructure Build-Out

Gartner also noted that **AI-driven physical infrastructure build-outs (data centres) are generating a secondary growth factor in employment for construction and maintenance workers.** This diversifies the employment story beyond software and IT roles, though it may concentrate economic benefits in regions hosting major data centre development.

## Broader Job Market Transformation

In late 2025, Gartner projected that roughly **32 million jobs a year will be 'reconfigured, redesigned, or fused by 2028'**, underscoring that the AI transition involves far more than simple creation and destruction—it requires continuous upskilling and role adaptation across the workforce.
