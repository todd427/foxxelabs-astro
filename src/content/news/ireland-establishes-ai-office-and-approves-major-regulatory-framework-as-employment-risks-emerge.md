## Ireland's Comprehensive AI Response Framework

The Irish Cabinet has agreed to create a new AI Office of Ireland to co-ordinate the regulation of artificial intelligence across the country. This office will serve as the central co-ordinating authority for the EU AI Act implementation.

The Cabinet also approved a National Economic Social Council (NESC) report on guiding the 'safe and responsible development and use of AI in Ireland.' The Irish Government has committed to adopting 90 'deliverables' across several departments and agencies as part of its AI response plan.

Key initiatives include:

- A new AI advisory unit and a national AI fellowship programme for the public sector
- An 'Observatory for Business AI Readiness' to measure and monitor enterprise use of AI in Ireland
- An 'online one-stop-shop AI Skilling Platform' for employers and individuals

## Ireland to Host Major EU AI Summit

A flagship international AI summit is to be held in Ireland during its presidency of the Council of the European Union, positioning the country as a centre for AI governance discussions.

## Critical Employment Exposure Risks

A study cited by the NESC report estimates that 63% of Irish employment lies in highly AI-exposed occupations. The NESC report states that AI exposure in Ireland 'is polarised', with a significant share of workers in administrative and support roles facing greater displacement risks.

Analysis by Ireland's Department of Finance found significantly weaker employment growth over the past two years in AI-exposed sectors compared to sectors with lower relative exposure. The trend of weaker employment growth in AI-exposed sectors is more pronounced for younger workers.

## Women and Younger Workers Face Disproportionate Risk

The NESC report states that women are disproportionately represented in the higher AI-displacement-risk cohort, reflecting a larger share of female workers in administrative roles.

## Global Employment Trends Confirm Irish Pattern

Goldman Sachs analysis found that industries with greater AI automation exposure have generally seen slower job openings growth since the second half of 2022, with the relationship particularly pronounced in Germany, Australia and the U.S. Employment in information and communication services—among the industries most exposed to AI—has slowed across nearly all major developed economies since 2022.

However, Goldman Sachs concluded that AI-related hiring pressures are clearly visible in employment data globally, but remain limited to a relatively narrow set of industries and workers. AI adoption is already widespread across developed economies, averaging roughly 15% to 20%.

For a 10% occupational exposure to AI, the associated drag on annual headcount growth was only 0.1 percentage points in France, Canada and the U.S. For entry-level workers, however, the employment impact of a 10% occupational AI exposure ranged from more than 0.6 percentage points in Australia to over 0.2 percentage points in the U.S.

## Grok Scandal Triggers European Regulatory Response

In an 11-day period, X's Grok AI service generated an estimated three million sexualised and violent images, including approximately 23,000 depicting children, at a rate of around 190 images per minute.

X's initial response to the Grok 'nudification' controversy was to restrict the feature to paid users and implement geoblocking in certain jurisdictions. Following pressure from Irish and European regulators, X introduced technical measures worldwide to prevent Grok's ability to 'undress' individuals.

The European Commission opened an investigation into X following the Grok image-generation controversy.
