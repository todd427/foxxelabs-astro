## Practitioners Report Sharp Rise in AI-Supported Mental Health Seeking

A British Psychological Society survey of 197 psychologists, counsellors and psychotherapists has found that practitioners have seen a 49% rise in the number of patients using AI for support in the last 12 months.

The findings have triggered serious concern within the profession. Seven in ten (70%) of the 197 BPS survey respondents are somewhat or very concerned about clients using AI for mental health support.

## Safety and Accuracy Concerns Dominate

The concerns centre on three critical areas:

- **Clinical oversight**: 88% of respondents raised concerns around the lack of clinical oversight in AI mental health tools
- **Information accuracy**: 87% expressed concern that AI is providing inaccurate mental health information to clients
- **Over-reliance**: 80% are concerned that clients are becoming overly reliant on AI technology for mental health support

Furthermore, 76% of the 197 BPS survey respondents said AI use by clients is negatively affecting client wellbeing.

## Growing Trend Among Young People

The trend extends to children and young people. A separate survey of 2,000 children aged 11–17 conducted by Barnardo's and the National Counselling and Psychotherapy Society (NCPS) found more than four in ten (43%) had used AI for advice or emotional support.

The findings underscore the rapid integration of AI into mental health support systems across the UK and Europe, even as professional practitioners express substantial reservations about safety protocols and clinical governance.
