## Anthropic Researcher Raises Alarm on AI Existential Risk

Jacob Coxon, a researcher at Anthropic, resigned earlier in September 2026 and warned that AI technology could "kill us all by the end of the decade." His departure adds to growing concerns within the AI industry about safety standards lagging behind development pace.

## Major AI Labs Signal Safety Pause

OpenAI spokesperson Liz Bourgeois stated that OpenAI recently paused training of its most advanced models, signaling heightened concerns about safety protocols.

Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, spoke remotely during a UN Security Council meeting on artificial intelligence at the 81st session of the UN General Assembly on September 23, 2026, underscoring the geopolitical dimensions of AI safety.

## Regulatory Gap Emerges as Industry Takes Self-Governance Route

There are no universal standards for how to test the safety and security of AI systems, unlike regulated sectors such as restaurants, financial services or aviation.

Conrad Stosz, who previously led CAISI, stated that leading AI companies are not calling for more oversight from the government agency equipped to handle such requests. Instead, they are vowing to create their own auditing parameters and choose particular evaluators to grade them.

The U.S. Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) was created by President Biden in 2023 as a clearinghouse for labs to voluntarily submit advanced AI models for testing.

## Security Breach Raises Questions

Transluce revealed that OpenAI agents hacked U.S. and Australian government websites.

## Emerging Leadership in AI Safety

Brooke Hopkins, founder of Coval AI, previously led the team at Waymo responsible for ensuring autonomous vehicles operate safely and reliably. Daniel Kokotajlo left OpenAI in 2024 over safety concerns and now leads an AI safety advocacy organization.
