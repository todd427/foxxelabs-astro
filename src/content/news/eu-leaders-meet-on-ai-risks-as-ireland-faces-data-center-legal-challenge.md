## EU Summit Focuses on AI Risks

EU leaders held an informal meeting in Dublin on 13 November 2026 to discuss the future of AI. Geoffrey Hinton, the Nobel Prize-winning 'godfather of AI', addressed EU heads of state at the summit, alongside Arthur Mensch, chief executive of AI start-up Mistral.

According to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, Hinton set out the 'enormous risks' of AI, specifically citing 'runaway AI'.

## Legal Challenge to Ireland's Data Center Policy

Meanwhile, Ireland's rapid expansion of AI data center infrastructure faces mounting legal pressure. A High Court case has been brought by ClientEarth, Friends of the Irish Environment, and Friends of the Earth Ireland challenging Ireland's Commission for Regulation of Utilities (CRU) over new electricity grid connection rules.

The legal challenge targets the CRU's regulatory framework rather than individual data center projects. The CRU previously imposed strict grid connection requirements in 2021 that effectively prevented most new data centers from accessing the grid. Under revised rules introduced in December, new data center facilities must install generation or battery systems capable of meeting their entire electricity demand and supplying power to the grid when needed.

Ireland's revised data center policy permits non-renewable electricity use for six years before requiring facilities to source 80% of their energy from renewables, excluding backup generation.

## Ireland's Data Center Boom and Energy Strain

Ireland currently hosts approximately 89 data centers. Irish data centers accounted for 23% of metered electricity consumption in 2025, up from 5% a decade earlier. The data center sector's electricity demand now exceeds the electricity consumption of all urban households in the country.

This expansion presents a climate challenge. Ireland aims to reduce emissions by 42% by 2030 compared with 2005 levels, but current projections indicate a reduction of only 13%.

The pressure on Ireland's data center sector has already prompted international shifts: TikTok abandoned plans for another Irish data center in April, redirecting investment toward other European data storage projects.
