## Social Media's Positive Role in Adolescent Mental Health

A new review article published on 12 November 2026 in *Child and Adolescent Psychiatry and Mental Health* (BioMed Central / Springer Nature) examines the potential benefits and positive effects of social media use among adolescents—both those in good mental health and those living with psychiatric disorders or trauma history.

The narrative review, submitted on 24 June 2026 and accepted on 20 October 2026, is authored by Frulli et al. with lead author affiliation at the Dipartimento di Neuroscienze, Riabilitazione, Oftalmologia, Genetica e Salute Materno-Infantile (DINOGMI) at Università di Genova, Italy. Co-authors are affiliated with IRCCS Istituto Giannina Gaslini (Genova) and CoBTek at Université Côte d'Azur (Nice, France).

The review is published open access under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives 4.0 International licence (DOI 10.1186/s13034-026-01202-5) and forms part of a Springer Nature collection titled *Digitalization: Risks and Opportunities for Youth Mental Health*.

## Key Findings and Context

According to the authors, positive outcomes related to social media use are emerging alongside discussions on the possible integration of digital technologies into clinical care. The authors note that interest in the positive aspects of social media use was heightened after the social isolation enforced by the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Cognitive Impacts of Problematic Social Media Use Reported

In contrasting research, a study published in the American Journal of Preventive Medicine (November 2026, Volume 71, Issue 5) identified three problematic social media use trajectories in adolescents: no/low increasing, low-to-moderate, and moderate-to-high.

The study found that adolescents in the low-to-moderate problematic social media use trajectory scored lower on the Picture Sequence Memory Test (β = −0.10, 95% CI = −0.16 to −0.03, p = 0.003) and the Picture Vocabulary Test (β = −0.11, 95% CI = −0.16 to −0.07, p < 0.001) compared to those in the no/low increasing trajectory.

Those in the moderate-to-high trajectory showed steeper declines: Picture Sequence Memory Test (β = −0.24, 95% CI = −0.35 to −0.13, p < 0.001) and Picture Vocabulary Test (β = −0.17, 95% CI = −0.25 to −0.09, p < 0.001).

The study concludes that increasing trajectories of problematic social media use were associated with lower cognitive performance in the memory and language domains.
