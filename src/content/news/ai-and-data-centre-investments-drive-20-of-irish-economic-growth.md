## AI Drives Fifth of Irish Economic Growth

AI and related data centre investments accounted for around 20% of Irish domestic economic growth in the year to the end of June 2026, according to Goodbody analysis. This outsized contribution reflects the scale of hardware investment flooding into the country.

Imports of AI data centre technology equipment used for capital investment in Ireland reached €11.4bn in the 12 months to the end of June 2026. This represented 59% of Ireland's modified machinery and equipment investment for the period—a striking concentration of capital into a single technology category.

The growth reflects a dramatic expansion in AI and data-centre hardware trade. Semiconductor, processor, and server imports have more than doubled since 2019, reaching €40bn by the end of August 2026.

## Supply Chains Rebalance Toward Asia

The AI infrastructure boom is reshaping Ireland's import patterns. Asia is now supplying almost a quarter of Irish goods imports, up from around an eighth in 2020, underscoring the geographic concentration of semiconductor and hardware production.

## Data Centres Consume Nearly a Quarter of Irish Electricity

Data centres accounted for 23% of Ireland's total electricity consumption in 2025, according to the Central Statistics Office. This rising energy footprint is becoming a material consideration for Irish infrastructure and policy planning.

## Employment Impact Remains Mixed

Goodbody found only a weak relationship between AI exposure and employment changes in Ireland to date. The firm noted weaker hiring of younger workers in information and communication technology in Ireland, while stopping short of attributing the decline solely to AI.

The employment picture contrasts with evidence of significant AI-driven productivity gains. Research cited by Goodbody from Trinity College Dublin found AI reduced meeting-related tasks by 72% and email-related work by 66%. Fifty-nine percent of organisations reported that AI saves them an hour a week, while 9% reported savings of more than five hours a week.

## Forecast Growth Ahead

Goodbody is forecasting Modified Domestic Demand growth of 3.2% for 2026, 3.5% for 2027, and 3.0% for 2028—underpinned by continued data centre expansion.

## Federal Reserve Convenes AI Experts

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh spoke at a Federal Reserve conference in San Francisco on Monday 17 November 2026 about AI's potential economic consequences. Warsh said the Fed has convened a group of AI experts to examine AI's implications for employment, productivity and central banking.

Warsh outlined a scenario in which AI fails to deliver anticipated productivity gains, causing equity valuations to fall, an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment, without ultimate productivity gains.

Warsh stated the Federal Reserve's role is neither advocacy nor opposition on AI, but to recognise the technology's widespread adoption will have significant economic impact with implications for monetary policy.
