## Enforcement Powers Now Live

As of 2 August 2026, the European Commission's AI Office holds formal investigative and enforcement authority over general-purpose AI model providers and the Act's prohibited AI practices. The stakes are significant: violations of general-purpose AI model provisions can incur fines up to the higher of €15 million or 3 percent of the provider's global annual turnover, while violations of prohibited practices provisions can reach up to €35 million or 7 percent of worldwide turnover.

Following these enforcement powers entering into application, the European Commission issued a first round of requests for information to different AI companies, focusing on safety, security, and copyright-related obligations.

## Von der Leyen Charts Industrial AI Strategy

European Commission President Ursula Von der Leyen's State of the Union speech provided the clearest articulation to date of the European executive's AI priorities: tailored industrial AI models in the sectors of health, transport, agri-food, advanced manufacturing, defence and space.

Von der Leyen also announced a discussion with frontier AI labs to support their efforts and future work with Canada and the United Kingdom on model evaluation, verification, early warning, and AI security.

## Eight Member States Demand Frontier Model Control

Eight EU member states — Finland, Denmark, Estonia, Germany, Ireland, Latvia, the Netherlands and Spain — endorsed a call for control of frontier AI models on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, signalling coordinated pressure for stronger governance at the bloc's highest level.

## New Protections for Minors

The European Commission published a proposal for an EU KIDS Act to protect minors from risky digital services and AI systems, including explicit obligations for AI companions and conversational chatbots interacting with minors. The proposal includes a prohibition on addictive designs in AI companions and chatbots interacting with minors, and prohibits the use of information from a minor's prior interaction in subsequent interactions.

## Transparency Code and Platform Designations

A code of practice on transparency for AI-generated content was signed by 190 organisations, including major providers. Meanwhile, the European Commission designated ChatGPT as a very large online search engine (VLOSE) under the Digital Services Act, bringing it under enhanced regulatory scrutiny.

OpenAI announced the expansion of advertisements on free and low-cost versions of ChatGPT across 31 European countries.
