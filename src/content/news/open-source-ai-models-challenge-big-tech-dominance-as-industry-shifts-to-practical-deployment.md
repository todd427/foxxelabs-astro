## Key Developments

The first week of March 2026 delivered a concentrated burst of AI releases that signal a fundamental shift in the industry landscape. Lightricks released LTX 2.3, a 22-billion-parameter open-source video model generating native 4K video at 50 FPS with synchronized audio. Meanwhile, Helios—a collaboration between Peking University, ByteDance, and Canva—achieves 19.5 FPS real-time video generation on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU under Apache 2.0 license.

OpenAI countered with GPT-5.4, delivering 33% fewer factual errors than its predecessor at $2.50 per million input tokens, available in Standard, Thinking, and Pro variants. Alibaba's Qwen 3.5 Small Series demonstrates that efficiency matters: their 9B parameter model matches models 13 times larger while running entirely on smartphones and laptops.

## Industry Context

This wave represents more than incremental progress—it marks the democratization of AI capabilities previously exclusive to trillion-dollar companies. As TechCrunch analysis reveals, 2026 is transitioning "from brute-force scaling to researching new architectures, from flashy demos to targeted deployments."

The infrastructure is maturing too. Anthropic's Model Context Protocol (MCP), now donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, is becoming the "USB-C for AI" that connects agents to real systems. OpenAI and Microsoft have embraced MCP, suggesting agentic workflows will finally move from demos to daily practice.

## Practical Implications

For Irish and European businesses, this shift toward smaller, specialized models offers significant opportunities. AT&T's chief data officer Andy Markus notes that "fine-tuned SLMs will be the big trend and become a staple used by mature AI enterprises in 2026, as the cost and performance advantages will drive usage over out-of-the-box LLMs."

With Ireland's CASPIr supercomputer arriving in 2027 and the new AI Office establishing regulatory frameworks by August 2026, Irish companies will have both the infrastructure and governance structure to leverage these advances responsibly.

## Open Questions

While open-source models are closing the capability gap, questions remain about long-term sustainability and support. Can smaller organizations effectively deploy and maintain these complex systems? How will the regulatory landscape, particularly Ireland's AI Act implementation, impact adoption timelines?

The industry is "starting to sober up" from the hype cycle, but the practical deployment phase brings its own challenges around integration, security, and governance.
