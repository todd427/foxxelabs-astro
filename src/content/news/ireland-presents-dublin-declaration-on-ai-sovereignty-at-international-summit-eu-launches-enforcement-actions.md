## Dublin Declaration Unveiled During Irish EU Presidency

On 14 October 2026, Ireland presented the Dublin Declaration on Reliable, Secure and Sovereign AI at the International AI Summit held at the RDS in Ballsbridge. The summit, attended by approximately 1,500 people, took place as part of Ireland's Presidency of the Council of the European Union.

Henna Virkkunen, European Commission Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy, delivered the opening address. Among the speakers were Demis Hassabis, chair of Google DeepMind, and Brad Smith, president of Microsoft.

## Declaration Structure and Status

The Dublin Declaration is structured around six lines of action covering AI adoption, skills training, model and infrastructure security, and digital infrastructure. However, it is a political declaration of intent, not binding law. No funding amounts, implementation deadlines, or legal commitments have been publicly confirmed. As of 14 October 2026, the full list of EU member states that formally endorsed the Dublin Declaration had not been published.

## EU AI Office Launches Enforcement Actions

Virkkunen announced that the European AI Office has begun its first enforcement actions under the AI Act. The office is requesting information from providers of advanced general-purpose models about security practices, external evaluations, and ongoing monitoring.

Providers of general-purpose AI models that fail transparency and safety obligations under the EU AI Act face fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover. Prohibited AI practices can draw penalties of up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover.

## Compliance Timeline Adjustments

An AI Omnibus package, adopted in June 2026 and in force by late July 2026, pushed back certain high-risk AI compliance timelines.

## Irish User Trust in Generative AI

Deloitte's 'Trust in GenAI: An Irish perspective' report found that 59% of surveyed Irish users said generative AI produces reliable results. However, 67% of surveyed Irish users identified privacy and security as essential factors in trusting generative AI tools, and 65% expressed concern about data privacy and the misuse of personal data.
