## A New Focus on Cyber-Threat Psychology

Ibrahim Mukherjee, a PhD researcher in AI at Brunel, University of London, published a blog post titled 'CyberPsychology: Keeping bad actors out' on The Times of Israel on 17 November 2026 at 6:57 AM. The piece explores how understanding psychological vulnerabilities can strengthen defenses against cyber threats.

Mukherjee, who founded Fahm.uk—the UK's first 'Sovereign AI' initiative—and runs two companies, Erasys (behavioural biometrics) and SanRa (cybersecurity), combines his MSc in Psychology and CISO qualification to bridge technology and human behavior. He was voted Outstanding Innovator of the Year 2025 by the AI Journal.

## Key Psychological Vulnerabilities in Cyber Fraud

The APA published 'Why We Keep Falling for Fraud' in Monitor on Psychology, volume 57, issue 7, in October 2026. The article identifies 'truth bias'—the tendency to initially treat information as credible, particularly when distracted, hurried or operating with limited information—as a core vulnerability exploited by bad actors.

Critically, the APA warns that there is no single personality profile that guarantees vulnerability or immunity to fraud. This challenges assumptions that only certain individuals are at risk.

A further concern: traditional fraud warning signs such as poor spelling are becoming less useful due to AI-generated content, making psychological defenses increasingly important.

## The Evolving Landscape of Cybersecurity Behavior

Research by Angela Sasse, cited in the APA's 'The Psychology of Cyberthreats' (published in Monitor on Psychology, volume 50, issue 2, in 2019), challenges the assumption that users are simply careless about cybersecurity. This reframes the conversation from individual blame to systemic design and education.

John Suler's research on the 'online disinhibition effect' (published in CyberPsychology & Behavior, volume 7, issue 3, pages 321–326, in 2004) established foundational understanding of how online environments change human behavior—knowledge now essential for designing fraud-resistant systems.
