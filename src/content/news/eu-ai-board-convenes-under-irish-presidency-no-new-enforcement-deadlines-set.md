## Ninth EU AI Board Meeting Held Under Irish Presidency

The ninth meeting of the European Artificial Intelligence Board convened under the Irish Presidency of the EU Council. The published agenda covered Commission enforcement priorities, transparency measures, market-surveillance cooperation, and frontier-AI cyber testing.

Despite its focus on enforcement, the meeting identified no adopted rule, new company obligation, or revised compliance date. The public account of the meeting did not name an enforcement case, affected provider, investigation timetable, or coordinated supervisory action.

## Board Structure and New Observer Status

The EU AI Board includes representatives from every member state and is supported by the AI Office as secretariat. The Board advises on implementation, guidelines, and draft delegated or implementing acts, and coordinates national competent authorities.

Moldova attended the meeting as an observer for the first time.

## Enforcement Discussion Focus

The September Board meeting's enforcement discussion covered Commission activities and priorities, recent AI incidents, new frontier-AI capabilities, and further coordination among member states.

## Key Transparency and Cybersecurity Initiatives

The agenda included the possibility of a secondment programme involving market-surveillance authorities and the European Data Protection Supervisor.

The EU Agency for Cybersecurity and the Joint Research Centre have been assigned the task of creating a secure platform for testing AI in cybersecurity, including through simulated environments. However, the September Board meeting did not publish a mandatory benchmark, certification threshold, or test deadline for frontier AI model providers in relation to cybersecurity.

## Critical Compliance Timeline

AI Act transparency obligations under Article 50 became applicable and enforceable on 2 August 2026. Providers of AI systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to meet the marking and detection obligation under Article 50(2).

Annex III high-risk AI system rules apply from 2 December 2027. Rules for high-risk AI systems embedded in regulated products apply from 2 August 2028.

## Parallel EU AI Office Investigations

The EU AI Office opened its first round of formal investigations in June 2026, targeting AI systems in three categories: hiring tools, credit scoring systems, and student monitoring applications.

As of 2 August 2026, the European Commission acting through the European AI Office holds formal investigative and enforcement authority over general-purpose AI model providers.
