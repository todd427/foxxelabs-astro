## Major report: problematic internet use demands global action

The Lancet Psychiatry Commission has published a report in *The Lancet Psychiatry* journal calling for global action on problematic internet use (PUI), describing it as 'highly prevalent, to be increasing and to entail considerable psychological and societal harms'.

The Commission is a multidisciplinary group including experts in addiction, public health, and policy. According to their findings, PUI can range from mildly impairing to 'clinically disordered'.

## Scale of the problem

Recent estimates suggest problematic internet use could affect more than 10 per cent of the world's population. The Commission has modelled the economic impact: if approximately 5 per cent of people in the UK were affected by PUI, it could cost the UK between £10 billion and £12 billion (€14.4 billion) a year.

## What counts as problematic internet use

The Commission classifies online gambling as an addictive behaviour. Problematic pornography use, compulsive health searching ('cyberchondria'), and compulsive shopping are classified as impulsive or compulsive behaviours.

## Mental health links

According to the Commission's report, it is 'probable' that depression, anxiety, attention disorders, and sleep disturbance can be both causes and consequences of PUI. The report also states that PUI 'predicts' future poor mental health outcomes including suicidal ideation.

## Platform design features under scrutiny

The Commission identified 'addictive' platform design features — including infinite scroll, personalised algorithms, and intrusive notifications — as designed to bypass human self-regulation and maximise time-on-site.

## Irish expert voices caution

Brendan Kelly, professor of psychiatry at Trinity College Dublin, cautioned against framing an increasingly broad range of human behaviours as health problems. Kelly argued there is a risk of 'medicalising social, educational and commercial problems for which medical interventions might not be the most appropriate solutions'.
