## CADA Draft Report Presented to European Parliament

A joint draft report on the European Commission's proposed Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA) was presented at the European Parliament on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. The proposal marks a significant step in the EU's effort to reshape its digital infrastructure landscape.

## Core Objectives

The CADA draft report calls for common EU rules on cloud and AI infrastructure and seeks to reduce the EU's dependence on foreign cloud providers. The initiative also emphasises greater support for European technology companies.

## Market Concentration Challenge

According to the European Commission, three non-European hyperscale cloud providers currently control more than 70% of the European cloud market—a concentration the proposal aims to address through targeted investment and regulation.

Under the European Commission's CADA proposal, the EU aims to triple its data centre capacity over the next five to seven years.

## Infrastructure and Standards

The CADA draft report seeks to establish common EU standards for cloud services and strengthen protections for critical sectors, including healthcare, energy and transport.

The report calls for faster approval procedures for new data centres, while ensuring that projects respect environmental requirements and benefit local communities.

## Supporting European Providers

The CADA report wants public authorities to buy more cloud services from European providers, creating market demand that supports the growth of homegrown alternatives.

## EU Updates Digital Services Act Election Guidelines

The European Commission opened a public consultation on draft updated guidelines for online platforms on protecting elections under the EU's Digital Services Act. The public consultation closes on 23 November 2026.

The updated DSA election guidelines focus specifically on risks linked to generative artificial intelligence and apply to 'Very Large Online Platforms' and 'Very Large Online Search Engines' that meet size thresholds under the Digital Services Act.

The EU's DSA election guidelines were first published in 2024. The proposed update builds on two years of experience gathered under the Digital Services Act and the EU's Code of Conduct on Disinformation.

EU rules under Article 35 of the Digital Services Act already require covered platforms to assess and mitigate risks to election integrity while safeguarding freedom of expression and information. The updated guidelines include measures intended to enhance protections for political candidates.
