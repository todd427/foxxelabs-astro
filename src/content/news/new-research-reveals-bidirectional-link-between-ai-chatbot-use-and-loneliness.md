## AI Companionship and Loneliness: Key Findings

Researchers Dunigan Folk and Elizabeth Dunn have published groundbreaking findings on how artificial intelligence shapes human connection. Their study, published in *Psychological Science*, examined the relationship between social chatbot use and loneliness using a 12-month longitudinal design across more than 2,000 adult participants recruited from four Western countries.

The research uncovered a bidirectional pattern: increased social chatbot use predicted increased loneliness, as measured by a single-item measure of emotional isolation. Conversely, feeling less socially connected predicted subsequent increases in social chatbot use.

However, the findings come with an important caveat. Chatbot use did not significantly predict decreases in the broader, more stable measure of social connection. Folk and Dunn urge caution in drawing strong conclusions from these findings, citing the exploratory nature of the analyses.

## Understanding AI's Role in Human Relationships

Complementing this empirical work, researchers Ryan L. Boyd and David M. Markowitz have introduced the machine-integrated relational adaptation (MIRA) model to describe when, how, and why AI functions as a relational entity in human ecosystems.

The MIRA model distinguishes two key roles of AI: as a relational partner (direct-interaction companion) and as a relational mediator (shaping human-to-human communication). The model identifies four principles of AI's relational impact: linguistic reciprocity, psychological proximity, interpersonal trust, and relational substitution versus enhancement. This framework, published in *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, provides a theoretical foundation for understanding AI's complex effects on human psychology.
