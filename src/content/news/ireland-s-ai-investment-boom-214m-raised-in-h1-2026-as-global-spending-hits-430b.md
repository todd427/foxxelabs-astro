## Irish AI Investment Accelerates

More than $250 million (€214 million) was invested in Irish AI companies in the first half of 2026, according to reporting from The Irish Times. This represents a significant injection of capital into Ireland's artificial intelligence ecosystem during a period of unprecedented global AI spending.

## Global AI Capital Reaches Record Levels

Global venture capital investment in AI reached approximately $430 billion in the first half of 2026. The pace of investment is accelerating dramatically: AI spending in the April-to-June 2026 quarter alone exceeded the $254 billion invested in the whole of 2025.

This trajectory is being driven by major technology companies. Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta are expected to spend between $490 billion and $520 billion in 2026. Their combined AI spending trajectory puts the industry on track for an annual run-rate of close to $1 trillion.

## Irish Enterprise Adoption Growing but Lagging Leaders

Enterprise AI adoption in Ireland more than doubled between 2023 and 2025, reaching 20 per cent of businesses. However, Ireland's adoption rate is in line with the EU average but behind Denmark (42%) and Finland (38%).

## Notable Irish AI Funding Rounds

Several Irish companies secured significant investments in the first half of 2026:

- **H&MV** (data centre company): €750 million in further investment
- **Fonoa** (tax automation): $110 million
- **Equal1** (quantum computing): $60 million
- **CameraMatics** (fleet safety tech): €49 million

## EU AI Act Enforcement Begins

From 2 August 2026, the European Commission's AI Office, together with national authorities, began enforcing the EU AI Act. New transparency requirements took effect immediately:

- Chatbots and other interactive AI systems must tell users they are dealing with AI, not a human
- Deepfakes (images, videos, or audio edited or generated using AI) must be labelled
- AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks so it can be detected more easily

The European Commission published a first list of more than 180 organisations that have signed the Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content.
