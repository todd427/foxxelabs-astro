## Historic Multi-Stakeholder Agreement on AI Safety

The 'Paris call for AI safety and security' declaration was signed on 9 November 2026, bringing together six governments, ten AI companies, and four civil society organisations in a coordinated commitment to AI safety and security standards.

### Government and Industry Signatories

Six governments signed the declaration: Bahrain, Canada, Finland, France, Norway, and the Netherlands.

Ten AI companies signed the declaration: Anthropic, Black Forest Labs, Cohere, Domyn, Hugging Face, Microsoft, Mistral AI, OpenAI, Sakana AI, and Synthesia.

Four civil society organisations signed the declaration: LawZero (Yoshua Bengio), SaferAI (Henry Papadatos), AI Safety Connect (Nicolas Miailhe), and Ifri (Laure de Roucy-Rochegonde).

Notably, the United States government, the Chinese government, Google, and Meta did not sign the declaration.

### Three Areas of Consensus

The declaration identifies three areas of consensus: the need for a clear regulatory framework; urgency of achieving results in transparency, incident reporting and risk management; and the necessity of implementing rigorous evaluation standards.

### Risk Management and Evaluation Framework

The declaration's framework for evaluation and risk management focuses particularly on mitigating dangerous misuse in the cyber and CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) domains.

The declaration names the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science (NAAIMES) as a vehicle to make evaluation methods and results more comparable and accessible across regions.

Signatories commit to publish non-sensitive evaluation findings in a standardised format in a publicly accessible shared repository, encompassing red-teaming protocols, benchmarking tools, risk-assessment, and stress-test results.

### Developer Commitments and Safeguards

Frontier model developers who signed the declaration commit to implement fail-safe mechanisms to shut down model operations within their control, including during training and internal use.

The declaration explicitly acknowledges the risk posed by the removal or circumvention of safeguards, including 'abliteration', in the context of open-weight model development.

### Whistleblower Protections

Policy makers who signed the declaration commit to offer secure and confidential channels for whistleblowers, including employees, to raise concerns about systemic AI risks without fear of legal or professional retaliation.

### Openness to Further Endorsements

The declaration remains open to new endorsements and is being updated.

### Connection to Prior International Efforts

The declaration references prior international AI safety efforts including the Hiroshima process, the Frontier Safety Commitments of the Seoul AI Summit, the G7 Evian AI meeting, and a United Nations Security Council meeting on AI held on 23 September.
