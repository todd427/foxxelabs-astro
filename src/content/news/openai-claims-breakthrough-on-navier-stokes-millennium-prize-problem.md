## OpenAI's Navier–Stokes Preprint

OpenAI has published a preprint claiming that the Navier–Stokes equations — which describe the motion of fluids — can break down. The preprint, which has not been peer reviewed, describes a spinning fluid system that ramps up to infinite speeds in a finite amount of time.

The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem was on the official list of Millennium Prize challenges.

## Competition and Collaboration

OpenAI researcher Sébastien Bubeck stated his team was spurred on by rumours that researchers at Anthropic were close to a solution of a Millennium Prize problem.

Meanwhile, mathematician Tristan Buckmaster worked on the Navier–Stokes problem with Levent Alpöge, a researcher affiliated with Anthropic, as a "purely personal collaboration."

## Google DeepMind's AlphaGenome Atlas

Google DeepMind released an atlas of the human genome called the AlphaGenome Atlas, charting the effects of nine billion single-DNA-letter changes — every possible mutation of this kind — in the human genome. The AlphaGenome Atlas uses predictions generated by a DeepMind AI model to chart these nine billion single-DNA-letter changes.

## AGI Testing Framework

Demis Hassabis, co-founder of Google DeepMind, suggested that testing whether an AI trained on information up to 1911 could reproduce Einstein's general theory of relativity could be "a good test for AGI."
