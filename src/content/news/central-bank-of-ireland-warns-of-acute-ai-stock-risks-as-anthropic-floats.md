## Central Bank Flags AI Valuation Shock as 'Acute' Risk to Ireland

The Central Bank of Ireland published its semi-annual financial stability review on 19 November 2026, issuing a stark warning about Ireland's exposure to an AI stock market downturn.

Central Bank governor Gabriel Makhlouf stated that "stretched valuations alongside increased concentration of global portfolios in a small number of AI-related stocks are leading to acute repricing risks." Mark Cassidy, the Central Bank's director of financial stability, described an AI stock slump as an "acute" risk to Ireland specifically.

The review warned that a shock to AI valuations could simultaneously reduce multinational enterprise investment, corporation tax revenues, and private wealth. Central Bank officials emphasised that Ireland is particularly exposed to an AI stock shock given its heavy reliance on Big Tech groups for corporation tax and direct and indirect employment.

## Market Turbulence Signals Growing Pressure

The warning arrives as the tech-rich Nasdaq Composite index has fallen 12 per cent from an all-time high reached in early October 2026. The decline follows Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang's exit as chief executive, which contributed to a tumble in Nvidia's share price in November 2026.

On the same day as the Central Bank review, Anthropic floated on the Nasdaq following a $107.3 billion IPO and private stock placement, underscoring the continued capital intensity of the AI sector.

## Wider Global Risks: Debt Financing and Circular Structures

The Central Bank's review identified global financial risks as generally high due to increasing AI-driven debt financing, rising sovereign risk, ongoing geopolitical tensions, and elevated cybersecurity risks.

The review noted that investments by AI hyperscalers including Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle are increasingly being fuelled by debt, amid a sharp drop in free cash flows from the sector. The Central Bank also flagged complex and circular funding structures, where companies such as Nvidia provide funding to start-ups that then use some of that money to buy Nvidia's AI chips (GPUs).

## EU AI Dialogue Continues

The Central Bank's warnings coincide with continued high-level focus on AI governance. EU leaders held an informal meeting in Dublin on 13 November 2026 to discuss the future of AI, attended by Geoffrey Hinton, described as the Nobel Prize-winning "godfather of AI," and Arthur Mensch, chief executive of AI start-up Mistral.

The ECB governing council's next rates decision is scheduled for December 17th 2026.
