## Cybervictimization and Mental Health: New Canadian Evidence

A new study published in *Frontiers in Psychology* has analysed public-use data from 17,403 Internet users aged 15 years or older, drawn from Statistics Canada's General Social Survey Cycle 34 (collected April 2019 through March 2020, released 2023). The research, authored by Na Zhi, Yanhui Liu, and Yinglei He from Heilongjiang University, China, examines the mental health impact of cybervictimization and fraud exposure.

## Exposure Patterns Across the Population

Among the 17,403 respondents with valid exposure indicators, the weighted share exposed to neither cybervictimization nor fraud was 76.54%. Fraud only accounted for 14.99% of respondents; interpersonal cybervictimization only 5.79%; and both exposures 2.68%.

Daily Internet use varied across groups, ranging from 91.6% in the neither-exposed group to 97.9% in the both-exposure group.

## Mental Health Disparities by Exposure Type

Unadjusted fair/poor mental health was reported by 10.75% of the neither-exposed group, 11.67% of the fraud-only group, 19.58% of the cyber-only group, and 22.30% of the both-exposure group.

After extended adjustment (controlling for conventional victimization, offline stalking, and discrimination), fair/poor mental health was more prevalent in the interpersonal cybervictimization-only group, with a prevalence ratio of 1.30 (95% CI 1.05–1.62).

The fraud-only group's prevalence ratio for fair/poor mental health was 1.12 (95% CI 0.93–1.36) after extended adjustment, with the confidence interval including the null.

In the extended model, the both-exposure (cybervictimization plus fraud) group's prevalence ratio for fair/poor mental health was 1.38 (95% CI 0.99–1.94), with the confidence interval including the null.

## Life Satisfaction Declines Across All Exposed Groups

Life satisfaction remained lower in every exposed group after extended adjustment: mean difference −0.23 (95% CI −0.36 to −0.10) for fraud only; −0.38 (−0.56 to −0.19) for cyber only; −0.31 (−0.58 to −0.05) for both exposures.

## Discrimination and Interaction Effects

The weighted prevalence of discrimination was 18.2% in the neither-exposed group, 37.4% in the cyber-only group, and 48.2% in the both-exposure group.

Formal interaction tests (multiplicative and additive) did not establish synergistic excess burden in the both-exposure group beyond the independent associations of cybervictimization and fraud: ratio of prevalence ratios for fair/poor mental health was 0.94 (95% CI 0.61–1.47).

However, in a restricted low-other-adversity sensitivity analysis (excluding respondents with any conventional victimization, offline stalking, or discrimination), the both-exposure group had a fair/poor mental-health prevalence ratio of 1.92 (95% CI 1.04–3.52).

## Survey Response Rate

The provincial response rate for the Statistics Canada General Social Survey Cycle 34 (Canadians' Safety) was 36.4%.

## Context: Youth Cybervictimization Rates

A separate 2019 Statistics Canada survey of adolescents aged 12–17 found a past-year cybervictimization proportion of 27.2% (95% CI 25.8–28.6) among those aged 15–17.
