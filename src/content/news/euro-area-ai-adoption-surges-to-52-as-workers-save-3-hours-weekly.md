## Rapid Growth in AI Workplace Adoption

The share of euro area workers using AI on the job has surged dramatically, according to the European Central Bank's Consumer Expectations Survey. The adoption rate doubled from 26% in 2024 to 41% in 2025, then climbed further to 52% in 2026—signalling a fundamental shift in how Europeans work.

## Frequency and Time Savings

Among those using AI tools, a majority of euro area workers deploy them around three days per week on average. The median worker using AI reports saving three hours per week, which represents approximately 7.7% of median working time—a material productivity gain that could translate into significant economic benefits across the region.

## Sharp Divides by Education and Age

AI adoption is far from uniform. Highly educated euro area workers reached 61% adoption, compared with just 37% among those with lower levels of education—a 24-percentage-point gap that underscores emerging digital inequality. Age also matters significantly: younger euro area workers are around 20 percentage points more likely to use AI at work than their older colleagues.

## High-Value Uses Remain Niche

The largest AI time-saving gains come from generating or debugging code, yielding nearly eight hours per week for those who use it. However, only around 8% of workers employ AI for this purpose, suggesting that the highest-value applications remain concentrated among specialists and developers rather than spread across the broader workforce.

## Productivity Gains Still Uncertain

While worker-reported time savings paint an optimistic picture, current literature estimates for additional annual productivity growth from AI over a ten-year period range between 0.1% and 3.4%—a wide band that reflects lingering uncertainty about how workplace AI adoption will translate into sustainable economic gains.
