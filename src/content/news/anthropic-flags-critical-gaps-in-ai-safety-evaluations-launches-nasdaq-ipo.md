## Research Paper Flags Safety Evaluation Gaps

AnthropIC published a research paper on November 13, 2026, flagging significant gaps in current AI safety evaluations. The paper argues that single-prompt tests and refusal rates may not reveal how models behave in real-world situations, exposing a critical weakness in industry-standard testing approaches.

Anthropic recommends several methodological improvements. Rather than judging individual responses in isolation, the company advocates evaluating the complete conversation and giving a model a failing grade if it produces an inappropriate response at any point during a simulated interaction. The researchers also recommend involving both technical and subject matter experts—including psychologists, biologists, and political scientists—in safety evaluations, depending on the risk being examined.

The paper further recommends using multi-turn simulations in which one AI model simulates the user and responds to answers generated by the model being tested, allowing conversations to develop differently across repeated tests.

Anthropic warns about two additional dangers. Benchmark contamination—test questions appearing in a model's training data—can allow a model to score well by recognition rather than demonstrating genuine safety. Reward hacking presents another risk, where an AI model finds a shortcut to achieve a passing score without demonstrating the behaviour the test is intended to measure.

In TASTE testing, the evaluated AI model agreed with experienced human researchers in 60 per cent of cases.

## Industry Safety Approaches

Other major players are strengthening their internal safety evaluations. OpenAI combines automated tests with expert reviews under its Preparedness Framework to assess model safety risks. Google DeepMind uses automated safety evaluations alongside manual red-teaming by specialist teams, with its Gemini model documentation reporting separate assessments of content safety and advanced capabilities.

## Anthropic IPO and Safety Concerns

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on November 10, 2026, targeting a listing on the Nasdaq Global Select Market. On November 13, 2026, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei told investors at a JPMorgan Chase fireside chat in New York that Anthropic is 'very paranoid' about AI safety.

Anthropic's IPO prospectus warns that increasingly autonomous systems could 'take harmful actions independently and at scale without human intervention.'
