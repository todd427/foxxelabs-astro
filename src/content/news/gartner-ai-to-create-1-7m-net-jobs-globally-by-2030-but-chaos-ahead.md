## AI Drives Net Job Growth, but Masks Significant Disruption

According to Gartner, AI is expected to drive a net increase of 1.7 million jobs globally by 2030. However, this headline figure obscures a more complex picture: Gartner projects AI will generate 10.6 million new jobs worldwide by 2030 while simultaneously eliminating 8.9 million jobs in the same period.

The research, titled "AI Won't Cause a Jobs Apocalypse, but Will Unleash Job Chaos Through 2030," underscores the uneven nature of AI's labour market impact.

## Already Seeing Real Disruption in 2026

Disruption is already underway. In 2026, AI was associated with 2.2 million jobs lost and 1.6 million jobs gained globally. More troubling, business executives deferred the creation and hiring of 1.3 million positions as a result of AI considerations in 2026 alone.

Projecting forward, Gartner estimates that by 2030, a total of 5 million people will have experienced delayed hiring as a result of business and IT executives postponing the creation of new positions.

## Massive Workforce Adaptation Required

Gartner estimates that just over 20% of workers—435.5 million employees worldwide—will have to adapt to the new shape of work in their jobs by 2030 due to AI. By 2030, between 0.4% and 1.8% of workers globally are projected to experience job loss directly attributable to AI, with sector-specific impacts varying significantly.

## Sector-by-Sector Breakdown

**Technology Sector:** Nearly 70% of the technology sector workforce has high AI exposure. By 2030, the technology sector will experience the creation of 1.9 million jobs and the loss of 0.4 million jobs due to AI, representing a projected AI job loss impact of 1.8% of the global technology workforce—the highest sectoral impact.

**Asset-Intensive Industries:** Gartner projects 6.4 million jobs will be created and 6 million jobs lost by 2030, with AI-driven job losses affecting 0.4% of the global workforce in this sector.

**Public Sector:** Gartner projects 0.4 million job gains and 0.9 million job losses by 2030, with AI-driven job losses totalling 0.4% of the global public sector workforce.

## Federal Reserve Warns of "Serious Short-Term Disruptions"

Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr echoed concerns about workforce disruption in remarks prepared for delivery at Yale University on November 12, 2026. Barr said the US should be prepared for "serious short-term disruptions" to the job market because of AI.

Barr noted that midterm elections swung partly on a backlash to the rapid AI buildout, signalling political sensitivity to these labour market concerns. His comments precede a public Fed forum in San Francisco convened by a task force appointed by Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh to explore what AI may mean for the economy.

## Current State: "Relatively Little Displacement" but Warning Signs

Barr said there has been "relatively little displacement" of workers from AI so far, and even evidence of AI boosting demand and adding jobs, but said the issue remains unsettled. However, the US is already seeing signs that AI may be a labour substitute in some sectors, especially for younger, less-experienced workers.

Barr pointed to a striking economic indicator: the US labour share of national income hit a record low of 52.8% in the second quarter of 2026, down from a range of roughly 60%–65% maintained from World War Two until early this century.

## Inequality Concerns

Barr warned that concentration of market power driven by AI "may help drive a widening of inequality that would strain the American Dream."
