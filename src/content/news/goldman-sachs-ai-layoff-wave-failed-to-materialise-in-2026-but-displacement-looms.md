## The AI Layoff Wave That Didn't Happen—Yet

Goldman Sachs economists have documented what many feared would not occur: despite widespread predictions of an AI-driven employment crisis at the start of 2026, the layoff rate remained historically low throughout the year, according to their 2027 U.S. outlook.

However, the reprieve may be temporary. Goldman Sachs expects a "slightly softer low-hire, low-fire form of full employment" to last one more year before AI displacement begins to have a larger impact in subsequent years.

Goldman economist Joseph Briggs estimated in October 2026 that up to 9% of workers could be displaced during the AI transition—roughly one in 11. Critically, Briggs found that automation-related labor disruption is limited when the overall economy is healthy but accelerates during cyclical slowdowns.

## Labour Market Remains Resilient

Several data points underscore the current strength of the job market. U.S. employers made 5.4 million hires in September 2026, a hire rate of 3.4%, while layoffs held at 1.6 million, or 1% of payrolls, according to the Labor Department's JOLTS report.

Initial U.S. jobless claims rose by 9,000 to 200,000 in the week ended November 7, 2026, with the four-week average flat at 195,000, which Goldman described as remaining at its lowest level in the series' history. Weekly U.S. jobless claims had touched 189,000 in mid-July 2026, the lowest since 1969, according to Reuters.

Goldman Sachs's 2027 outlook forecasts U.S. GDP growth of 2.4% in 2027, which it described as above consensus, and does not forecast a recession.

## Longer-Term Displacement Projections

While 2026 saw no mass layoffs, projections for the coming decade paint a different picture. The McKinsey Global Institute projected that 6 million to 16 million U.S. workers may need to switch occupations by 2035, approximately 7% of current employees at the midpoint of that range.

Stanford's Digital Economy Lab, in an August 2026 update, found that employment for workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed jobs was about 19% below where it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, up from 15% a year earlier. Importantly, Stanford's Digital Economy Lab stated that AI-related labour market adjustment appears to operate primarily through reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations. The Lab stated they do not see widespread, economy-wide job displacement associated with AI.

## The Cautionary Voice

Not all observers remain optimistic. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei predicted in May 2025 that AI could wipe out half of entry-level white-collar jobs and push unemployment to 10% to 20% within one to five years.
