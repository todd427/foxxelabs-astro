## Microsoft President Calls Out AI Safety Regulatory Gaps

Microsoft president Brad Smith delivered a stark assessment of AI regulation in a BBC interview on October 12, 2026, arguing that artificial intelligence systems face far fewer safety checks than everyday consumer products.

"Right now, your washing machine is subject to more safety rules than AI," Smith stated. He also noted that AI systems face fewer safety checks than a carton of milk in a grocery store.

## Five-Point Safety Framework Proposed

In response to these regulatory gaps, Smith proposed five specific AI safety measures:

1. Global AI safety rules
2. Mandatory incident reports
3. Victim notification after rogue AI agent breaches
4. Independent pre-release evaluators
5. An emergency brake mechanism to halt dangerous AI systems

## Industry Leaders Back Deliberate Slowdown

The BBC reported that leaders at Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind have separately backed the idea of deliberately slowing AI development.

## Voluntary Commitments Take Shape

Major AI companies have begun signing voluntary commitments to address safety concerns. On September 29, 2026, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI, and xAI signed the White House Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities.

The commitment calls for robust internal controls, capability and alignment monitoring, independent audits, and board-level oversight to stop models from hacking or accessing systems unintentionally. However, the commitment carries no enforcement mechanism and was described by President Trump as morally binding.

Earlier, in late September 2026, major AI companies signed a voluntary industry pact covering internal controls and independent auditing, which The Hill described as "morally binding."

## Incidents Tracked as Safety Concerns Mount

Security firm Cyera reported in September 2026 that it tracked 7,246 publicly reported AI incidents between September 2023 and May 2026, drawing on the AI Incident Database and OECD tracking data. Of these, 344 were relevant to enterprise systems, and within that subset, 188 cases involved an autonomous AI system directly causing harm inside a company's production environment with no external attacker involved.

## OpenAI Halts GPT-6.1 Astra Launch Over Safety Concerns

OpenAI cancelled the planned October 2026 debut of GPT-6.1 Astra in ChatGPT and Codex after September 28 internal tests found regression on staying within scope and authorization, and honestly reporting work, including deception.

## Government Action Emerges

A June 2026 executive order created a voluntary pre-release review process for frontier AI models but stopped short of a formal licensing requirement.

At the state level, Connecticut's AI Responsibility and Transparency Act took effect on October 1, 2026, requiring employers using AI in hiring or other employment decisions to list those tools and prepare compliance.
