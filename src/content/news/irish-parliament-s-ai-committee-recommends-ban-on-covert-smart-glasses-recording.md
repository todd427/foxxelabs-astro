## Report Published with 64 AI Recommendations

The Joint Oireachtas Committee on Artificial Intelligence published its Third Interim Report on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The report explores issues around AI, Truth and Democracy, as well as AI-generated non-consensual sexual imagery, including that of children. It contains 64 recommendations addressing risks to Irish society from artificial intelligence.

## Smart Glasses Recording Ban Recommended

Among its recommendations, the committee calls for smart glasses capable of covert recording without consent to be prohibited. The report also recommends that the Data Protection Commission take up the smart glasses privacy issue.

Smart glasses are spectacles or sunglasses that can capture images and video footage discreetly and hands-free, with a small LED light usually the only way for someone to tell that they are recording. Meta produces smart glasses in partnership with Ray-Ban.

The Data Protection Commission raised concerns about an earlier version of Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses in 2021, stating it had not been demonstrated that comprehensive field-testing had established whether the indicator light was an effective way of notifying people they were being recorded. In its 2023 annual report, the DPC stated that Meta had made changes to Ray-Ban smart glasses following its engagement, including increasing the size of the indicator light and adding a blinking pattern when recording.

The recommendation gained momentum after Labour Senator and committee member Laura Harmon raised the smart glasses issue in the Seanad in May 2026 following reports that women in Cork had been filmed without their consent, including one woman who was receiving medical care.

## AI in Public Services and Child Safety

The report recommends that consideration be given to "strongly regulating AI in public services, not relying on it." Where AI adoption in public services is deemed beneficial or necessary, the report recommends retraining public servants, including a cohort who would "actually understand how the AI black box or algorithm produces a particular result."

On child safety, the report recommends that AI products presenting risks to children's safety or rights should be subject to legally required independent assessment before deployment.
