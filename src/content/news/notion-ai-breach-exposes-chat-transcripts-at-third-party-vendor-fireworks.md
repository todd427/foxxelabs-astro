## Security Incident Confirmed

Notion confirmed that a security incident at Fireworks AI exposed Notion AI chat transcripts linked to fewer than 1% of its customer workspaces.

## Timeline of Events

Fireworks first notified Notion on the evening of November 2, 2026, that a small dataset had been accessed by malicious actors. By the morning of November 3, 2026, Notion disconnected its systems from Fireworks, deleted all data in the Fireworks system, and stopped all communication between its systems and Fireworks.

On November 6, 2026, Fireworks notified Notion that malicious actors may have accessed potential customer data. The final impacted dataset was confirmed on November 8, 2026, which included Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature.

## Affected Features and Services

Notion used Fireworks AI to run open-weight models inside its product and for Insights classification jobs. Following the confirmed breach, Notion disabled its Insights feature and stopped serving models through Fireworks.

## Contractual Commitments

Fireworks had contractually committed to zero data retention for Notion data prior to the breach.

## AI-Related Threats Now Top Cyber Concern in Ireland

The findings align with rising anxiety across Irish organisations about AI security risks. According to the EY Ireland Cyber Leaders Index 2026, which surveyed 161 cybersecurity professionals and senior leaders across Ireland, AI-related threats now outweigh employee-related concerns as the leading cyber worry for organisations.

### Key Findings

35% of Irish cyber leaders identified AI-related threats as their biggest cyber concern. Additionally, 47% of Irish cyber leaders said rapid advances in AI and the heightened threat environment are making it harder to maintain control.

Moreover, 22% of Irish cyber leaders specifically identified the threat from rogue or compromised AI models being used for hacking as their top concern.

### Investment Priorities Shifting

28% of Irish organisations surveyed said they have moved part of their cybersecurity budget to support AI adoption following recent incidents involving trusted or autonomous AI systems.

Five of the top six future investment priorities for Irish organisations relate directly to AI, led by AI governance, risk management and oversight at 41%. Controls to prevent or contain rogue AI behaviour was cited as a top investment priority by 34% of Irish cyber leaders surveyed, while continuous security testing of AI systems was cited as a top investment priority by 30% of Irish cyber leaders surveyed.
