## Young Europeans Increasingly Relying on AI for Mental Health

A survey conducted among 3,800 young people aged 11 to 25 across France, Germany, Ireland, and Sweden has revealed concerning trends in how European youth approach emotional wellbeing. Nearly half of the surveyed young Europeans rely on AI chatbots when experiencing emotional stress or anxiety.

The findings raise significant questions about trust and privacy in digital mental health support. According to the research, 69% of young people surveyed believe that AI can offer reliable advice. Even more striking, 56% of young surveyed Europeans feel that AI chatbots can keep a secret and won't share intimate details with other parties.

These statistics underscore a potential gap between young people's confidence in AI systems and the actual safeguards protecting their sensitive personal information. Marie-Laure Denis, President of the CNIL, will be watching these developments closely as European regulators grapple with how to protect minors in digital spaces while balancing innovation.

## New Cyberpsychology Journal Issue Addresses AI and Digital Wellbeing

Timing the broader conversation around digital mental health, Cyberpsychology: Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace published Issue 4, Volume 20 on 4 September 2026, featuring 15 new articles. The issue covers topics including artificial intelligence and synthetic relationships, digital privacy, content moderation, media multitasking, and digital inclusion.

All articles published in Cyberpsychology are open access, making research on these critical topics freely available to academics, policymakers, and practitioners across Europe and beyond. The journal, which achieved an Impact Factor of 3.708 as announced in its June 2022 issue, continues to be a leading venue for rigorous scholarship on how technology shapes human behaviour and wellbeing.

Recently, Emmelyn Croes, an Assistant Professor at Tilburg School of Humanities and Digital Sciences at Tilburg University, joined Cyberpsychology as Associate Editor on 7 October 2025, strengthening the journal's editorial capacity to examine these emerging challenges.
