## AI-Driven Workforce Reductions Double in One Year

European employers are cutting staff at an accelerating pace due to AI adoption, according to Littler's 2026 European Employer Survey. The share of employers that have made workforce reductions as a result of actual or anticipated increased AI use has more than doubled since Littler's 2025 survey, rising to 28% from 13%.

The trend extends to hiring freezes as well. The share of European employers that have reduced hiring due to AI also doubled, rising to 32% from 13% in the prior year's Littler survey.

The survey draws on responses from nearly 500 business executives, in-house lawyers and HR leaders across Europe, 51% of whom hold C-suite positions.

## Widespread AI Adoption in HR, But Compliance Gaps Remain

94% of respondents in the Littler 2026 European Employer Survey report using AI in HR or employment law functions. Yet compliance preparation is fragmented: 93% of respondents have taken at least one step to prepare for the EU AI Act, but no single step has been taken by a majority of respondents.

Only a quarter of survey respondents have assigned internal ownership or responsibility for EU AI Act compliance—a significant gap given that 67% of respondents expect regulatory changes related to AI to affect workforce management over the next 12 months, up from 58% in 2025.

## Pay Transparency Compliance Complicated by Uneven Implementation

Most EU member states missed the June 2026 deadline to transpose the EU Pay Transparency Directive into national law. The fallout is clear: 84% of Littler survey respondents say uneven country-by-country implementation has complicated their compliance planning.

More than half (52%) of survey respondents expect pay equity and transparency regulation to affect workforce management in the next 12 months, a 10-percentage-point jump from last year.

## Employees Fighting Back With AI-Assisted Complaints

Nearly three-quarters of European employers (74%) report a rise in employees using AI to prepare or support workplace complaints, grievances or legal claims compared to the previous year.

Employers are responding in kind: approximately half (49%) of employers who reported a rise in AI-assisted employee complaints are responding by using AI tools themselves to review or respond to such complaints.

Among employers whose workforce actions affected current staff, 48% report that employees used AI to draft responses or counterproposals.

## Broader Tech Sector Downsizing Continues

The trend reflects wider technology sector turbulence. In July 2026, Microsoft eliminated slightly over 2% of its worldwide workforce. In October 2026, Microsoft offered buyouts to around 60,000 employees to contain costs.

Across the entire technology sector, there have been more than 200,000 layoffs in 2026. Meta has reported reductions in employment in Ireland in 2026.

## Economic Pressure Driving Workforce Changes

Nearly 9 in 10 (88%) Littler survey respondents took at least one workforce-related action in response to economic pressure or business challenges over the past year. 32% of respondents increased their use of automation or AI specifically to reduce labour needs.

Meanwhile, the EU has postponed the AI Act's rules for high-risk uses until December 2027, leaving employers in a holding pattern as they balance rapid AI deployment against incomplete regulatory frameworks.
